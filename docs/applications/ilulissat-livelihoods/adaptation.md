# Greenland/Ilulissat Livelihoods

[Back to the adaptation overview]({{ site.baseurl }}/applications/ilulissat-livelihoods/)

## System or topic

This adaptation explores the community of livelihoods in Ilulissat, Greenland, and the connections between livelihoods, infrastructure, society and the environment.

It was developed for use in Avannaata Kommunia and initially applied during the Ilulissat Science Forum 2025. Although tailored to this regional context, it could be adapted for other communities with relatively minor changes.

## Purpose

The adaptation was developed as a collaborative systems-mapping tool for exploring climate resilience within Avannaata Kommunia.

It supports a participatory mapping process in which community members and polar researchers visualize important connections between local livelihoods, infrastructure, environmental conditions and society.

The adaptation can serve two related purposes:

### Community mapping

Local residents can use the cards to map relationships within their community and explore how the resulting system might respond to scenarios introduced through disruption cards.

### A collaborative bridge

The game can bring local residents and polar researchers into a shared discussion. It allows researchers to learn about the complexity and interconnectedness of community livelihoods and infrastructure, while contributing scientific information about where climate-driven changes might weaken, break or alter connections within the system.

By bringing these perspectives together, the process is intended to support a more climate-resilient community and a scientific community that is better informed about local concerns, knowledge and priorities.

## Card categories

The system cards are divided into four categories.

### Livelihood and community

These cards represent local livelihoods, activities, social conditions and aspects of community life. They include fishing, hunting, tourism, employment, multiple income sources, education, health and wellbeing, local traditions, migration, caregiving, children and young people, and collaboration within the community.

### Climate and environment

These cards represent environmental conditions, species and seasonal processes relevant to Ilulissat. Topics include snow and sea-ice seasonality, winter and summer conditions, changing season lengths, fish and shellfish populations, whales, polar bears, rainfall and mosquitoes.

### Infrastructure

These cards represent physical infrastructure, services and connections supporting the community and its livelihoods. They include transportation, the airport and port, food supply, buildings, roads, hotels, restaurants and cafés, waste management, energy production, mining, and protected or recreational areas.

### Society, economy and governance

These cards represent economic, political and institutional conditions. Topics include fishing and hunting quotas, Inuit rights, land-use planning, national legislation, international agreements, markets, profitability and costs, subsidies and compensation, and trust between actors.

### Disruption cards

A separate group of cards introduces climatic and societal changes that may affect the mapped system. These include extreme weather, permafrost thaw, thinner sea ice and ice-free fjords, sea-level change, warming, changing season lengths, the green transition, geopolitical tensions, and pandemics and related restrictions.

The Ilulissat Science Forum workshop used only the climate-related disruption cards.

## Languages

The cards are available in:

- English
- Kalaallisut

The English and Kalaallisut cards are provided in separate printable files.

For bilingual use during the Ilulissat Science Forum workshop, corresponding English and Kalaallisut cards were taped together. This allowed local residents and visiting researchers to use the same cards and supported communication and knowledge sharing in a multilingual group.

The Kalaallisut translation was prepared by **Kasper Kristensen and Nukaaka Lund-Mathæussen**.

## How the general tool was adapted

The general Systems Card Game was adapted by selecting and formulating cards representing the community of livelihoods in Ilulissat and the wider context of Avannaata Kommunia.

The cultural adaptation was undertaken by **Kristiina Verro, Marie Stougaard and Nukaaka Lund-Mathæussen**.

The broad categories of the general tool were populated with locally relevant cards concerning fishing, hunting, tourism, community life, seasonal environmental conditions, important species, infrastructure, governance and economic conditions.

A separate set of disruption cards was developed to introduce climate-driven and other significant changes. These cards allow participants to explore how particular developments might affect relationships within the community system.

The workshop process was organized around three future-oriented stages:

1. Participants create a shared map of the local livelihood and community system.
2. They explore how a climate-related surprise or disruption might affect the mapped system and discuss possible responses.
3. They reflect on personal and collective aspirations and discuss desirable futures for the community.

Participants can create additional cards when the prepared set does not adequately represent their experiences or concerns.

## Guidance for reuse

The adaptation is designed for facilitated discussions in groups of approximately five to eight people.

During the mapping stage, participants take turns selecting two to four cards that they consider important. The group arranges the cards into a shared map and uses markers to show connections between different elements. Participants can create additional keyword cards whenever an important element is missing.

Once the system map has been created, the group introduces a disruption card. Participants discuss:

- what the disruption could mean in the local context;
- which elements and connections it might affect;
- where connections might weaken, break or change; and
- how the community could cope, adapt and prepare.

In the final stage, participants reflect on personal dreams and aspirations connected to their livelihoods. These perspectives provide a basis for discussing desirable futures and the changes that could help the community move towards them.

The cards can be used in groups consisting primarily of community members or in mixed groups of local residents and researchers. For bilingual groups, corresponding English and Kalaallisut cards can be attached to one another.

One general facilitator should open and close the event, keep time and guide the overall agenda. Each table should have a facilitator who supports the conversation, explains the exercises and ensures that everyone has an opportunity to contribute.
