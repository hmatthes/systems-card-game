# About the Systems Card Game

The Systems Card Game is a participatory method for making the structure and relationships within an operational system visible.

It can be used to explore systems such as livelihoods, communities, occupations, or other settings in which people, resources, environmental conditions, infrastructure, institutions, and decisions interact. Operational systems of non-human actors can be considered as well - one adaptation of the method is about frogs and their environments!


## Development of the method

The method began with Surprises and Dreams - an initial participatory workshop format for mapping livelihoods, exploring disruptions, and articulating desired futures. See Backstory for more details and acknowledgements. 

It has since been developed into a more general tool that can be adapted to different systems, questions, and workshop settings.


## What the tool supports

The card-based approach helps participants to:

- identify key elements of a system;
- discuss how those elements are connected;
- bring together different forms of knowledge and experience;
- examine how external pressures or unexpected events affect the system;
- explore possible responses and adaptations;
- articulate aspirations and desired futures, and what in the system needs to change, so that first steps towards those desired futures can be taken.

The physical arrangement of the cards creates a shared cognitive map that participants can discuss, revise, and use as the basis for further exercises.

Method supports open, constructive discussion, while making tensions between various aspirations visible. It is easy to learn and use, and it produces relevant material for research and decision-making.


## Backstory

The Systems Card Game started with Surprises and Dreams workshops, and reindeer husbandry card set. These were developed iteratively by a multidisciplinary research team throughout 2022. The deck evolved from handwritten cards made with a blank magician’s deck into a professionally printed card set available in several languages. 

During years 2020-2025 several research projects dealing with reindeer husbandry in northern Fennoscandia were active: EU Horizon -funded CHARTER, and three national ones in Finland, POVAUS, POMURI and CLIMINI. All these needed better understanding about the operational environment of reindeer husbandry. How does the livelihood system work? What are the core elements of the system? How are different global pressures like climate change and climate change mitigation shaping the operational environment of the livelihood? And most importantly, what are the desired futures – aims and dreams – of practitioners of this livelihood? 

In addition to these research questions, there was the methodological question – how to co-produce answers to these questions in participatory settings, in workshops? 
We started to read about Three Horizons framework (Sharpe et al. 2016) and got interested in methods that can support co-production of transformative knowledge. In these, the idea is to discuss systems knowledge (i.e. what is the world like where we live?) together with normative knowledge (i.e. where do we want to be in the future?). This way also discussions on transformative knowledge (i.e. how to get there?) are based on shared understanding.

We also learnt about earlier participatory methods, especially card-based ones, and those based on cognitive mapping (see for example Van Vliet et al. 2010). We needed a method that could combine mapping a system, discussing the impacts of various known drivers and also surprising developments, and aims and dreams of participants, related to topic discussed (in this case, reindeer husbandry).

Based on literature and earlier collaboration with reindeer herders and other actors in reindeer husbandry, we prepared a list of elements of the operational environment of reindeer husbandry. In April 2022, the list was used together with a student group at the Lapland University of Applied Sciences (Rovaniemi, Finland); core elements were selected from the list, and a deck of cards was made using marker pens, tape and normal playing cards. Then mapping of the system was piloted with this group of students. Impacts of climate change were also discussed, after mapping. Early version of the method was piloted also with a student group at the Sámi Educational Institute SOGSAKK in Inari, northern Finland, in May 2022. After this, attention was paid to ways to include “surprises” into the workshop method (joker cards), as well as “dreams”.

In August 2022, first full-scale Surprises and Dreams workshop was organized in Inari, northern Finland (see Rasmus et al. 2025, especially sections about development of the tool and practical application). After this, several workshops with reindeer husbandry actors, utilizing the method, have been organized in Finland and in Sweden (see “Adaptations and workshops”). New adaptations have also been seen for various settings, broadening the original idea to different regions, languages, communities and research questions. Surprises and Dreams has grown into a versatile participatory workshop method that can contribute to finding acceptable solutions to locally relevant problems.
